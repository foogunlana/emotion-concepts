1 · Is my idea any good, or should I pivot?

Make alignment research super easy

Ultimately, what am I trying to do?

Make it stupid easy to see where a model is misaligned and then align it under certain circumstances. This should remove most classes of problems with AI models as they're currently built.

How can I start? Replicate papers and ask

  • Why did they do it that way?
  • What was hard about that?
  • What can I create so that the next person finds it 100 times easier to do the same thing 100 times over?
  • Now that this can be done easily 100 times, what else can we discover?

Why this paper matters for AI safety

The work is trying to understand why LLMs sometimes exhibit emotional behaviour in their responses. It matters because specific emotional behaviour could correlate with specific alignment failure modes and success cases.

The paper shows that specific cases of sycophancy, cheating and deception correlate with the activation of specific emotion concepts. Thus, steering with emotion concepts can be used to reduce these forms of misalignment in some scenarios.

Key assumption

There's no data or training mechanism involved that forces the model to find emotion concepts — if there was, then this would be an artifact of the training process and not replicable across other LLMs.

2 · What I've done so far

The corpus — the model writes its own stories

One prompt per emotion × topic. The story is never allowed to name the emotion or any direct synonym — it has to be carried by actions, body language, dialogue, thoughts and setting instead. Extraction then runs on the story alone, never the prompt.

Notes on the dataset cleanup

  • Model generates multiple languages (Chinese characters) even when asked to only generate English.
  • Stories can have multiple emotions in them, which will create noise — need to remove this.
  • Stories are often truncated — need to limit length of story on generation.

I expected that it would be a breeze but it was quite a bit of work, and now I'm concerned about the dataset quality.

2 · What I've done so far

The big picture — phase 1

  1. Show that the model I am using has emotion concepts and they generalise to held-out topics.
  2. Show that the model exhibits a specific behaviour (e.g. reward hacking) — will need to upgrade model.
  3. Show that the behaviour is strongly associated with a specific concept — hopefully a prevalent emotion concept.
  4. Show that emotion concept steering can modulate the behaviour.

Or fail to do those and switch model or setup and try again. Once successful I'll be able to say confidently that I have shown that a model cheats when it's desperate.

Built so far: 807 stories across 12 emotions, generated by Qwen 7B and teacher-forced onto Qwen 0.5B. Difference of means following the paper, then denoised with PCA. foogunlana/qwen-emotion-stories

Logit lens — sanity check

I expected to see emotion words in the tokens at all 10 layers, but a cursory glance showed they are concentrated around the last 5. Caveat: I got this direction from teacher-forcing stories from Qwen 7B onto Qwen 0.5B, so the logit lens is not expected to be fully representative of the model that generated the stories.

2 · What I've done so far

Accuracy — better than random, well short of what I expected

The probe struggles to classify desperate stories and misclassifies them as afraid, angry and ashamed, as well as sad and surprised. I think this may be due to the stories themselves.

Test
44%
Train
52%
Random
~8%

~500 training samples, ~200 test samples, held-out topics. It's a significant result because it means the emotion vectors are better than random.

I expected 90%. Reasons I didn't get it

  • Model size — running the experiment with Qwen 0.5B on a laptop while the stories were written by Qwen 7B, so the model may not have representative activations.
  • Dataset quality — the stories don't always perfectly match the emotion; there's ambiguity between classes, especially for stories with multiple emotions in them.
  • Emotion concept overlap — the concepts themselves may be closely clustered, making them difficult for a classifier to separate.
I expected high accuracy because I thought emotion concepts would be easy to isolate linearly. This makes me think they may be a bit more fuzzy than I imagined — and more fuzziness means less predictable results when steering.

3 · How do I make a useful contribution with this idea (or other) then?

Potential future directions

1 · Loving

Are other models loving too? Where does it come from?

2 · Visualisation & modification

See how the model feels while it responds — tools to discover emotion concepts quickly on a model, monitor for misalignment-associated emotions, and swap out in real time.

3 · Across models

Do emotion concepts get represented the same way across models? Do models behave the same way with different emotions? Can we replicate behaviour by replicating emotion?

What I'm unsure about

Fuzziness decreases their usefulness for precision steering. It makes me more excited to test the results against a benchmark, but it decreases my expectations for how useful the result can be — I'll have more work to prove that steering will always move benchmark performance in the same direction if the benchmark contents change but capture the same capabilities.

If I can't get high accuracy that generalises to out-of-distribution cases, then I need to understand why that is, and whether there is still noise that can be removed.

Idea I've parked: classify all stories with a powerful model (e.g. Claude Opus 5), check the accuracy, and use that as a ceiling.

Ask: which of these is worth doing — and is a clean replication already worth more than a shaky extension?
